1929.

A young German doctor named Werner Forssmann has a dangerous idea.

He wants to find a safe way to reach the human heart.

His hospital says no, so he tries it on himself.

He cuts into a vein in his arm and he slides a thin tube inside.

Slowly, he pushes it about 65 centimeters, all the way into his own heart.

Then he walks to the x-ray room and he takes a picture to prove it.

His bosses are furious.

They call it a reckless stunt and he loses his job.

But the idea does not die.

Other doctors use it to study the heart and to save lives.

In 1956, Forssmann wins the Nobel Prize in Medicine.

The man they fired had invented modern heart catheterization.
