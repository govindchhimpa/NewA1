In 1933, an astronomer said the universe was hiding most of its weight.

Fritz Zwicky saw galaxies in the Coma cluster moving so fast they should have flown apart. His answer: invisible matter holding them together. Dark matter.

Nobody listened for almost forty years.

Then Vera Rubin measured spinning galaxies in the 1970s, and the stars moved the same impossible way.

Today, dark matter is thought to be most of the matter in the universe. Zwicky was not wrong. He was forty years early.
