On a quiet June evening in eighteen nineteen, the ground in western India rose up, and a wall of earth appeared where there had been none. People named it the Dam of God. It is still there today.

This is the Rann of Kutch, in Gujarat, right beside the border with Pakistan. Today it is a flat, white, salty wasteland. Every monsoon, shallow water covers it. Every dry season, it bakes into crust.

But in eighteen nineteen, this coast looked very different. The mighty Indus river reached the Arabian Sea through a maze of channels and creeks. One branch, called the Nara, flowed past a mud fort named Sindri, and out to the sea. People fished, farmed, and traded along it.

June sixteenth, about a quarter to seven in the evening. The earth begins to shake. It lasts two or three minutes. Scientists today estimate it at around magnitude seven point seven. At least fifteen hundred people die, and that only counts the larger towns.

Then the land itself starts to change.

Along a line of about eighty kilometers, the ground rises. A ridge of earth about six meters high pushes up, right across the path of the old river.

At the same time, the land around Sindri Fort sinks, by as much as three meters. The sea rushes in. The fort is wrecked and partly drowned, and survivors climb to the top of the ruins and wait.

In a few minutes, a river country becomes a salty lake of more than a thousand square kilometers. Sindri Lake.

And the new ridge gets a name. Allah Bund. The Dam of God.

Now the Nara has a problem. Its road to the sea is blocked. The water backs up behind the ridge, and a new lake forms to the north. For years, the river keeps pushing against the barrier. After eighteen twenty six, it begins to break through, and in time it cuts through the Allah Bund itself. Sindri Lake slowly turns fresh again.

And the Indus itself? There is evidence it shifted west after the earthquake, leaving its old delta as a landscape of marshes, creeks and salt lakes.

Look at the map today and you can still trace the story. A coast that became a lake. A lake that became a marsh. A marsh that became a desert of salt.

Mountains take millions of years to grow. The Dam of God took a few minutes.
