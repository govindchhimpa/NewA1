1950s.

Marie Tharp is drawing a map of the ocean floor.

Women are not even allowed on the research ships, so she works from data in an office.

She notices something strange.

Along the middle of the Atlantic Ocean, a huge valley runs down the center of an underwater mountain range.

To her, it looks like the Earth is splitting apart.

That would mean continents really do drift.

Her partner, Bruce Heezen, dismisses it.

He calls it girl talk.

But Tharp keeps checking.

Then, earthquake data lines up perfectly with her valley, and Heezen changes his mind.

Within a few years, scientists build the theory of plate tectonics.

Today, Marie Tharp's maps hang in museums, and they show the world beneath the sea for the first time.
