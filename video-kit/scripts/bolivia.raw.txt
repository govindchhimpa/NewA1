Bolivia has a salt flat so flat that it turns into a giant mirror. The Salar de Uyuni covers around 10,000 square kilometers, high in the Andes, more than 3,500 meters above the sea. After rain, a thin layer of water turns the whole ground into a mirror of the sky. Under the salt lies something even more valuable, lithium. It is one of the largest known reserves in the world, and lithium is used in the batteries of phones and electric cars. Here's the twist. Bolivia has no coast. It lost its seaside to Chile in a war in the 1880s. Would you visit the mirror? Tell me in the comments.
