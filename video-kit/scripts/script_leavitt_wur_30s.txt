Nineteen twelve. At Harvard, a woman measures stars on glass plates for thirty cents an hour.
Her name is Henrietta Leavitt, and she spots a pattern: the slower a star pulses, the brighter it truly is.
It becomes a ruler for the universe, but the paper goes out under her boss's name.
In nineteen twenty-three, Edwin Hubble uses her ruler to prove Andromeda is a galaxy beyond our own.
Leavitt never saw it. She died two years earlier.
Today it is called Leavitt's Law.
