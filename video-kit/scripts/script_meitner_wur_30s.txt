She explained how to split the atom, and someone else got the Nobel Prize.

In 1938, Lise Meitner fled Nazi Germany. Her old partner, Otto Hahn, sent her strange results: uranium had turned into barium. She and her nephew worked out why. The atom had split. They called it fission.

But the Nobel Prize for it went to Hahn alone.

Today, element one hundred nine is named meitnerium. The Nobel missed her. The periodic table did not.
