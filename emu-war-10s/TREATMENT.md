# The Great Emu War: treatment (10 s, 1930s Rubber Hose Cartoon)

Topic: the 1932 "Emu War" in Western Australia, when soldiers with machine guns failed to beat the emus.
Length 10.0 s, 1920×1080 (4:3 gate), 24 fps. English narration by a radio announcer, title-card subtitles.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **One gag, start to end.** A proud soldier on stage, his gun jams, an emu stretches its neck in and takes over the film. Setup, turn, payoff in ten seconds.
2. **A countdown.** "3 soldiers, 2 machine guns, 1 enemy..." numbers hop on the beat, then the enemy turns out to be a bird. Funny, but the numbers eat the reading time.
3. **A singalong card.** A bouncing ball over "The army went to war..." Charming, but ten seconds is too short for a verse and a payoff.

**Chosen: 1.** One subject, one turn, one native move, and it reads at thumbnail size. Opening image: an iris opening on a soldier who is far too proud. Ending: the emu grabs the iris and pulls it shut on the soldier, then the end card. The score is one 4-bar hot-band phrase that stops dead at the jam.

## 2. Logline and arc

A proud 1932 soldier sets up his machine gun to win the war on emus; the gun jams, and an emu stretches its neck in, honks in his face and closes the film on him.

- Setup (0–4.5 s): proud soldier, gun, title.
- Turn (4.5–6.5 s): rat-a-tat, jam, band stop, shocked pie eyes.
- Ending (6.5–10 s): the emu takes over, iris shut, "The End".

## 3. Benchmark

- **Learn from** early-1930s sound cartoons as a grammar: stage-wide proscenium staging, a hard cut to the reaction, the band stopping dead before the gag, the iris as a physical object. And from modern rubber-hose revivals: how a big band carries every action.
- **Do not take** any character, design, melody, title lettering layout or gag from those works.

## 5. Beat sheet (beat = 12 frames = 0.5 s, 120 BPM; final timings)

- 0.00–0.40 iris opens from black on the soldier's face (slide whistle up).
- 0.25 title "The Great Emu War" hops in letter by letter (woodblock ticks); 1.35 "1932" pops in under it.
- 0.40 voice L1: "Nineteen thirty-two! The army goes to war... with emus!" (3.5 s).
- 0.4–4.5 the soldier breathes on the beat, chest puffing every other beat; gun, crate and bush breathe in phase.
- 4.50 hard cut, slow push-in: three shots on 4.50 / 4.75 / 5.00 (snare + band stabs), recoil jitter, squeezed eyes.
- 5.50 jam: a spring pops out of the gun ("boing"), smoke puffs.
- 5.60 band stop: silence except the projector clatter; everything freezes. 5.75 pie eyes go wide, mouth "O", hat jumps.
- 5.90 voice L2: "Guess who won?" (the first sound after the stop).
- 6.50 the emu's head peeks in from frame right (woodblock); 6.60–7.25 its neck stretches across the frame (slide whistle + rubber creak), clarinet sneaks down.
- 7.25 honk in his face (trombone raspberry), he squashes back, his hat flies off.
- 7.50–7.75 the emu bites the iris edge and pulls it shut on him.
- 7.75–7.90 the end card rolls down like a window shade (ratchet), piano run up.
- 8.50 the emu pops up in the card's corner on xylophone; 9.00 band button and a wink; held to 10.0.

Voice: one American announcer (Kokoro), radio chain (HP 260 Hz / LP 4.6 kHz, saturation, compression), music ducked ~8 dB under it. Optical soundtrack pass over music and foley.

## 8. Subtitles and titles

- Title: "The Great Emu War" in Shrikhand, white with heavy ink outline and drop shadow, letters hopping in on eighth notes; "1932" in Limelight caps. Held 0.25–4.5 s.
- Subtitles: period title-card plates (IM Fell English SC, white on near-black, double-rule border, dot-and-ring corners), bottom of the gate: L1 0.4–4.5 s, L2 5.9–7.75 s.
- End card: "The End" in Shrikhand on a grey card, held 7.9–10.0 s.

Notes: 10 s is shorter than the style's usual 30–60 s, so the treatment uses one gag and two short lines; the title holds 4.25 s, slightly over the 4 s minimum.

## 9. Production notes

- Speech-to-text check: not run. The Whisper model downloads from Hugging Face, which this machine's network blocks; both lines were checked by length and listened to through the mix only.
- Mux: this machine has ffmpeg 6.x, which `core/render/mux.sh` refuses; `mux6.sh` does the same master (two-pass loudnorm to -14 LUFS, limiter, grain 3). Measured: -14.1 LUFS, peak -4.1 dBFS.
- Compared with the style's demo: structure, opening, signature shot, camera path and score shape differ; the ending (a character pulls the iris shut) is shared, so here the emu bites the iris edge and the card rolls down after it.
